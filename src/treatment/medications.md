## Low-dose naltrexone (LDN)

### What it is

Naltrexone at full dose (50 mg) blocks opioids and is used for alcohol and opioid dependence. At **low doses (0.5–4.5 mg)** it's used off-label in ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, Long COVID, MS and Crohn's disease.

### How it might work

1. Briefly blocking opioid receptors overnight triggers a **rebound rise in the body's own endorphins** the next day. People with ME/CFS have lower levels of beta-endorphin.{% ref "conti-1998" %}
2. It calms the brain's immune cells (**microglia**), which may reduce neuroinflammation.{% ref "younger-2014" %}
3. It **restores the TRPM3 calcium channel** in immune cells, according to Griffith University's lab work. See [The science](/understanding/science/#the-immune-system-and-trpm3-griffith-university).

### The evidence, honestly

| Study | Type | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Finnish series, 2019 (218 patients){% ref "polo-2019" %} | {% ev "open" %} | 74% reported a positive response. Symptoms returned when people paused and improved when they restarted. |
| Griffith preliminary data{% ref "nalcovid-anzctr" %} | {% ev "open" %} | 83% reported improvement (unpublished, cited in a trial registration). It justified running a placebo-controlled trial, but isn't evidence the drug works. |
| Irish pilot, 2022 (38 Long COVID patients started LDN){% ref "okelly-2022" %} | {% ev "open" %} {% ev "adjacent" %} | Improvements in fatigue, function and sleep |
| Griffith lab studies, 2021–2025{% ref "cabanas-2021", "eaton-fitch-2022", "sasso-2025" %} | {% ev "lab" %} | TRPM3 function restored. Patients reported improved thinking and immune symptoms, but **not pain, sleep or dizziness**. |
| Small fibromyalgia trials{% ref "younger-2013" %} | {% ev "rct" %} {% ev "adjacent" %} | Positive for pain and fatigue (31 people) |
| FINAL trial, Denmark (99 women with fibromyalgia){% ref "duebruun-2024" %} | {% ev "rct" %} {% ev "adjacent" %} | Missed on pain; some signal for memory |
| **Canadian post-COVID trial (UBC), May 2026**{% ref "nacul-nct", "sicktimes-berlin-2026" %} | {% ev "rct" %} {% ev "adjacent" %} | **Missed its main goal (reducing fatigue).** See below. |

### The Canadian trial

- Led by Dr Luis Nacul at the University of British Columbia and funded by the Canadian Institutes of Health Research and the UK ME Association.
- A Phase 2 trial in **post-COVID fatigue syndrome** (Long COVID with ME/CFS-type fatigue). **People who had ME/CFS before COVID were excluded.** 160 people enrolled; LDN was built up to 4.5 mg/day over 16 weeks. Registry: [NCT05430152](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05430152).
- **LDN did not reduce fatigue more than placebo.** Of 137 people analysed (66 on LDN, 71 on placebo), the fatigue endpoint was not met.{% ref "sicktimes-berlin-2026" %}
- **More than 40% of the placebo group felt better.** This is why open-label figures like "74%" or "83%" can't be taken at face value.
- Detailed results (e.g. on pain, sleep, dizziness and subgroups) aren't published yet. Figures reported so far come from conference coverage; the ME Association describes the preliminary results as negative and says they're being submitted for publication.{% ref "mea-ldn-2026" %}

### Why LDN is still worth discussing

- The Canadian trial only included **post-COVID** illness. People with classic ME/CFS, e.g. after glandular fever, may respond differently. It counts against LDN in Long COVID more than in ME/CFS.
- It measured **fatigue**. If LDN helps a subgroup with brain fog or sleep, a fatigue-focused trial could miss that.
- It was too small to detect a benefit in a subgroup.
- LDN is **cheap, generally safe and reversible**.

{% callout "note", "Our summary" %}
LDN is probably a small-effect drug overall, with a minority of clear responders we can't yet identify in advance. A **three-month personal trial with a symptom log** is reasonable to discuss with your doctor. Set expectations at "maybe", not "83% chance". Don't let anyone sell it as *the* answer.
{% endcallout %}

### Practicalities (Australia)

- **Prescription only.** Made by a compounding pharmacy, roughly $40–60 a month.
- **Commonly started low** (0.5–1 mg) and increased slowly, e.g. by 0.5 mg every 1–2 weeks, to 3–4.5 mg. That takes about 6 weeks.
- **Usually taken at night.** If it causes vivid dreams or insomnia (the most common side effects, usually temporary), switching to the morning often helps.
- **Judge after 2–3 months** at the target dose. A two-week pause afterwards shows whether it was doing anything.
- **It blocks opioid painkillers** (codeine, tramadol, oxycodone). Tell every doctor and dentist, and stop it before planned surgery as advised.
- Many GPs haven't prescribed it. See our [Find a doctor](/support/find-a-doctor/?tag=ldn) list of LDN prescribers, or ask **Emerge Australia**.
- Griffith University is starting a **placebo-controlled LDN trial (TreatMELC)** in Australia. See [trials](/research/trials/?country=Australia). Note that you're usually not eligible if you're already taking LDN.

## Low-dose rapamycin (sirolimus) {% ev "open" %}

- Rapamycin is an immune-modulating drug. At low weekly doses it's being studied in ME/CFS.
- **Simmaron Research (US):** of 70 patients who reached the first checkpoint, **52 (74%) improved** in fatigue, PEM and dizziness on standing, at 6 mg a week. It was generally well tolerated.{% ref "ruan-2025" %}
- **People whose illness started after an infection appeared to respond better**, which suggests it may suit infection-triggered or EBV-associated ME/CFS. This comes from the researchers' preliminary analysis rather than the published paper.{% ref "simmaron-rapamycin" %}
- **No placebo group**, so it needs a controlled trial. A placebo-controlled follow-up is planned for mid-2027.
- Rapamycin affects the immune system and needs monitoring. Some Australian specialists prescribe it.

## Pyridostigmine (Mestinon) {% ev "rct" %}

- In a placebo-controlled study of 45 people, a single dose of pyridostigmine **improved how the heart and circulation respond to exertion** in ME/CFS: peak oxygen use rose on the drug and fell on placebo.{% ref "joseph-2022" %}
- Most relevant for people with [dizziness on standing or POTS](/living/orthostatic-intolerance/).
- The **LIFT trial** (Brigham and Women's Hospital, Boston, with the Open Medicine Foundation) is testing it alone and together with LDN.{% ref "lift-nct" %}

## Low-dose aripiprazole (Abilify) {% ev "open" %}

- At a **tiny fraction** of its psychiatric dose (0.2–2 mg a day), a Stanford clinic review found **74% of 101 patients** improved in fatigue, brain fog, sleep and PEM.{% ref "crosby-2021" %}
- But in a large patient survey (3,925 people), more than half reported a positive effect while more than a fifth reported a negative one, and loss of effect over time is commonly reported.{% ref "eckey-2025" %}
- The first placebo-controlled trial (**PAIS-AriSE**, Charité Berlin) is due to start in late 2026.{% ref "pais-arise-nct" %}

## Guanfacine + NAC {% ev "adjacent" %}

Used for brain fog. A small Yale case series in Long COVID (12 people, 8 improved) found benefit.{% ref "fesharaki-2022" %} It's cheap and available, but some people with ME/CFS report crashing on it.
