## What long COVID is

The World Health Organization defines **post COVID-19 condition** as symptoms that occur "usually 3 months from the onset of COVID-19", last "at least 2 months", and "cannot be explained by an alternative diagnosis". Symptoms can be new or persisting, and can fluctuate or relapse.{% ref "who-pcc-2021" %} The Australian Government adopted this definition in 2024 and prefers the term **post-acute sequelae of COVID-19 (PASC)**, while acknowledging that people use "long COVID" for any symptoms lasting more than 4 weeks.{% ref "gov-response-2024", "pasc-plan-2024" %}

More than 200 symptoms have been described. The most common are **fatigue, breathlessness and cognitive dysfunction (brain fog)**.{% ref "who-pcc-2021", "healthdirect-lc" %} There is no test for long COVID.

## Long COVID and ME/CFS

Long COVID is an **umbrella term**. Some people have lasting lung, heart or clotting problems. Others, especially those with **post-exertional malaise**, have an illness indistinguishable from ME/CFS.

- In the largest population-based study (US RECOVER, 11,785 infected adults), **4.5% met ME/CFS criteria** after COVID, compared with 0.6% of uninfected people.{% ref "vernon-2025" %} {% ev "observational" %}
- In clinic and community samples of people already diagnosed with long COVID, the proportion meeting ME/CFS criteria is much higher: **45–59%** in Berlin cohorts and 58% in a US community survey.{% ref "kedor-2022", "jason-dorri-2022", "meruk-lc-prevalence" %}
- Griffith University found that people with long COVID and people with ME/CFS had **the same quality-of-life and disability scores** over 12 months, both far below healthy controls.{% ref "griffith-lc-2024" %}
- The Australian Government's 2026 research round now specifically funds studies **comparing PASC with ME/CFS, POTS and fibromyalgia**.{% ref "mrff-2026" %}

Emerge Australia's position is that management should be the same: **pacing, rest and stepwise symptom management**, with a warning that graded exercise can harm people with PEM.{% ref "emerge-lc-position" %} See [After COVID vs glandular fever](/understanding/triggers-compared/) and [ME, CFS, POTS & related conditions](/understanding/related-conditions/).

## How many Australians have it?

The honest answer is that nobody knows precisely, because Australia has no national registry and the studies use different definitions:

| Source | Estimate |
|---|---|
| AIHW literature review (2022){% ref "aihw-longcovid-2022" %} | About **5–10%** of Australian COVID cases had symptoms lasting more than 3 months |
| Western Australia survey, Omicron wave (11,697 adults){% ref "woldegiorgis-2024" %} | **18%** reported long COVID 90 days after infection; 71% of those had fatigue, 60% concentration problems. Critics note the low response rate could inflate this. |
| Modelling by UNSW (2024){% ref "costantino-2024" %} | **310,000 to 1.37 million** Australians (1.2–5.4% of the population) at the September 2022 peak, falling to 170,000–870,000 by late 2024 |
| Melbourne Alfred Health post-COVID cohort{% ref "holland-2024" %} | Of those who responded, 60% had at least one persistent symptom and **15% had not returned to work or study** |
| Young Victorians aged 15–29 (2023 survey){% ref "engelberts-2025" %} | **11%** reported long COVID; only about 1 in 10 had a formal diagnosis; 79% reported difficulties with work or study |

In other words: somewhere between a few hundred thousand and over a million Australians, with women, people aged 40–69 and people with pre-existing conditions at higher risk.{% ref "woldegiorgis-2024" %}

## What the government has done

In April 2023, a parliamentary inquiry published **"Sick and tired: casting a long shadow"**, with nine recommendations including a national database, evidence-based guidelines co-designed with patients, a coordinated research programme, better vaccination and antiviral access, GP-led care, indoor air quality standards, and funding for ME/CFS research and support.{% ref "inquiry-2023" %}

The Government response in February 2024:{% ref "gov-response-2024" %}
- **Adopted the WHO definition** and released a **National PASC Plan** built around GP-led care, with a longer (60+ minute) Medicare consultation item and MyMedicare registration.{% ref "pasc-plan-2024" %}
- Committed **$50 million** over five years through the Medical Research Future Fund for long COVID research, with grant rounds in 2023, 2024 and 2026.{% ref "mrff-2023", "mrff-2026" %}
- **Did not fund** public hospital long COVID clinics (left to the states) or commission new clinical guidelines.
- Provided **$880,000** to Emerge Australia for ME/CFS and long COVID patient support.

**Guidelines:** Australia's COVID-19 living guidelines, including advice on care after COVID, lost funding in 2023 and were **retired in 2026**.{% ref "livingevidence-covid" %} As of September 2026 there is **no current Australian clinical guideline for long COVID**. GPs rely on RACGP resources, the NSW Agency for Clinical Innovation's clinical practice guide, and international guidance.

## Where to get care

**Most public long COVID clinics have closed.** About 23 clinics opened during the pandemic; by late 2023 the Royal Australasian College of Physicians reported that nearly all metropolitan clinics had shut and none operated regionally, and by November 2025 only a few public services remained.{% ref "racp-clinics-2023", "abc-clinics-2025" %} St Vincent's Hospital Sydney, which treated more than 1,000 patients, closed its clinic in September 2025.{% ref "abc-stvincents-2025" %}

**Your GP is the front door.** The National PASC Plan is built around GP-led care.{% ref "pasc-plan-2024" %} Useful tools:
- A **GP Chronic Condition Management Plan** (which replaced GP Management Plans in July 2025) gives Medicare-subsidised allied health visits, e.g. an occupational therapist for pacing.{% ref "mbs-ccm-2025" %}
- The **long consultation** item for complex appointments.
- **Telehealth** for follow-ups.
- Ask for the same [tests](/treatment/testing/) and [POTS assessment](/living/orthostatic-intolerance/) as for ME/CFS.

## Australian research you can join

Australian long COVID research is growing thanks to the MRFF funding. Studies and trials with public contacts include:

- **NALCOVID (Griffith University):** placebo-controlled trial of low-dose naltrexone for long COVID brain fog, sleep and pain. Recruiting Australia-wide by post; (07) 5678 9283. See [trials](/research/trials/#nalcovid).
- **ADAPT (St Vincent's Sydney / Kirby Institute):** long-term observational cohort; SVHS.ADAPT@svha.org.au.{% ref "svhs-adapt" %}
- **SAGE trial (UNSW, from 2026):** a $3 million trial of a scalable GP and allied health model for long COVID in primary care.{% ref "unsw-lc-2026" %}
- **HEAL APT (Burnet Institute):** a planned adaptive platform trial testing several treatments, in partnership with Emerge Australia and the Australian POTS Foundation; not yet recruiting.{% ref "burnet-heal" %}
- **STRONGER (George Institute):** atorvastatin for neurological long COVID; 410 participants; results pending.{% ref "stronger" %}
- The **CRE-PAVING** post-viral research centre runs an annual Long COVID Conference (1–2 October 2026, Brisbane) and lists recruiting trials.{% ref "cre-paving" %}
- Join the **AusME registry** and **OMF StudyME** to hear about new studies. See [Clinical trials & studies](/research/trials/).

## Reducing your risk

**Vaccination.** A 2025 meta-analysis of 31 studies found vaccination is associated with about **23% lower odds** of long COVID, and boosters lower it further, although the evidence quality is low.{% ref "green-2025" %} Large European cohorts found vaccinated people had roughly 30–50% lower risk.{% ref "catala-2024" %} In Australia, from 1 October 2026, free COVID-19 vaccines are only available to certain groups at higher risk of severe illness. Ask your GP or pharmacist whether you are eligible.{% ref "emerge-2026-vaccine", "health-vaccination" %} If you already have ME/CFS, discuss timing with your doctor, since some people react strongly.

**Antivirals.** Paxlovid does **not** treat established long COVID. Several randomised trials have found no benefit, including RECOVER-VITAL (959 people, 2026), which tested courses of up to 25 days.{% ref "geng-2024", "pax-lc-2025", "baden-2026-recover-vital" %} Whether taking it during the acute infection *prevents* long COVID is unclear, with mixed observational evidence and no trial in low-risk people. In Australia it's PBS-subsidised for higher-risk groups if started within 5 days of symptoms.

**Reinfection.** Each infection carries a fresh risk. In a UK community study, 4% developed new long COVID after a first infection and 2.4% after a reinfection.{% ref "ons-reinfection-2023" %} In children, reinfection roughly **doubled** the risk of a long COVID diagnosis.{% ref "zhang-2026-reinfection" %} For people who already have long COVID or ME/CFS, a new infection is the most common trigger for a serious relapse. Masks in crowded indoor spaces, ventilation and staying away from sick contacts are reasonable precautions. See [Pacing](/living/pacing/#avoiding-infections).

## Work, money and support

- **Sick leave:** full-time employees get 10 days' paid sick leave a year, which accrues; employers can ask for reasonable evidence.{% ref "fairwork-sick" %}
- **Flexible work:** after 12 months' service, employees with a disability can formally request flexible hours or location.{% ref "fairwork-flex" %}
- **Discrimination:** disability is a protected attribute under the Fair Work Act; Fair Work Infoline 13 13 94.{% ref "fairwork-discrim" %}
- **Income support:** see [Centrelink & the DSP](/support/centrelink/) and [ME/CFS and the NDIS](/support/ndis/). Long COVID is assessed under the same rules as ME/CFS.
- **Pacing** is the core self-management strategy. healthdirect's "3 Ps" are pace, plan and prioritise.{% ref "healthdirect-lc" %} See [Pacing](/living/pacing/).
