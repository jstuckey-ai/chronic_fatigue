## Can ME/CFS qualify?

**Yes, but it's assessed case by case.** The NDIS Act doesn't name conditions. To get in, you must meet five "disability requirements" (section 24 of the Act):{% ref "ndis-act", "ndis-disability-req" %}

1. You have a disability caused by one or more **intellectual, cognitive, neurological, sensory or physical impairments** (or a psychosocial disability)
2. The impairment is, or is likely to be, **permanent**
3. It **substantially reduces your functional capacity** in at least one of: communication, social interaction, learning, mobility, self-care, self-management
4. It affects your **capacity for social or economic participation**
5. You're likely to need NDIS supports **for your lifetime**

Plus you must be **under 65 on the day you apply** and an Australian citizen, permanent resident or Protected Special Category visa holder living in Australia.{% ref "ndis-age" %}

There is no NDIS policy statement about ME/CFS, and it isn't on the "likely permanent" condition lists that give some diagnoses fast-tracked entry. Emerge Australia has asked for it to be added.{% ref "emerge-ndis-position" %} Those lists are being phased out anyway in favour of assessments of function,{% ref "health-securing-ndis" %} so for ME/CFS the question has always been, and will remain: **how much does it limit what you can do, and is that permanent?**

### Fluctuating illness is not a barrier (in law)

Two lines in the NDIS Act matter a great deal for ME/CFS:{% ref "ndis-act" %}

> "An impairment or impairments that vary in intensity may be permanent ... despite the variation." (s 24(2))

> "An impairment or impairments that are episodic or fluctuating may be taken to be permanent ... despite the episodic or fluctuating nature of the impairments." (s 24(3))

And the current rules say an impairment "may be permanent notwithstanding that the severity of its impact ... may fluctuate or there are prospects that the severity ... may improve".{% ref "ndis-rules-2016" %} In practice, Emerge Australia reports that "some NDIS planners view ME/CFS as impermanent due to the fluctuating nature of symptoms ... or as health-related, not disability-related".{% ref "emerge-ndis-factsheet-2023" %} Your evidence needs to pre-empt that.

### What "permanent" means now

Under the current rules, an impairment is permanent "only if there are no known, available and appropriate evidence-based clinical, medical or other treatments that would be likely to remedy the impairment".{% ref "ndis-rules-2016" %} For ME/CFS there is no such treatment: no drug is approved, and [graded exercise therapy](/treatment/avoid/) is no longer recommended. Your doctor's letter should say so plainly.

### What "substantially reduced functional capacity" means

You meet this if, for an activity (say, self-care or mobility), you usually **need help from another person**, or **need assistive technology, equipment or home modifications**, or **can't do the activity even with help**.{% ref "ndis-rules-2016", "ndis-disability-req" %} From August 2026, functional capacity is legally defined as your ability to do an activity **without assistance** (or with only commonly used aids), judged "in a context that excludes, as far as possible" your environment and personal circumstances, and each activity is considered "as a whole".{% ref "ndis-amend-2026", "health-ndis-changes" %}

{% callout "tip", "Function on your worst days, done reliably" %}
Emerge Australia advises doctors to base their evidence on "your patient's worst day managing their ME/CFS symptoms",{% ref "emerge-ndis-gp" %} and argues that function should be assessed by "what a person can do safely, reliably and repeatedly, not what they can perform once", and that activities which trigger PEM or delayed deterioration must not be treated as evidence of capacity.{% ref "emerge-ndis-submission-2026" %} Being able to shower once and then crash for two days is not the same as being able to shower.
{% endcallout %}

## The changes coming

The NDIS is in the middle of its biggest overhaul since it began. Key dates:{% ref "health-ndis-changes", "health-securing-ndis", "ndis-framework" %}

| Date | What changes |
|---|---|
| **3 Oct 2024** | A legal list of what is and isn't an "NDIS support"; funding tied to the impairments that got you in |
| **1 Jan 2025** | New applicants receive an **impairment notice** listing the categories of impairment that met the access test{% ref "ndis-impairment-notice" %} |
| **27 Aug 2026** | New legal definition of **functional capacity** (above); tighter rules on unscheduled plan changes (only for "significant and ongoing" changes; the NDIA has 90 days to decide) |
| **1 Oct 2026** | **Budget cuts** to new and renewed plans: "social, economic and community participation" supports cut by 50%, "improved daily living skills" (where OT and other allied health sit) cut by 10%. **Not affected:** in-home help with daily living, personal care, cleaning and laundry, nursing and medication, mobility equipment and transport, home modifications, high-intensity supports.{% ref "ndis-budgets-2026" %} |
| **1 Feb 2027** | Tighter assessment of "reasonable and necessary" supports rolled out progressively |
| **1 Apr 2027 to 2030** | Existing adult participants move to **new framework plans** built on a standardised support-needs assessment by a trained NDIA assessor, with a total funding amount |
| **1 Jan 2028** | **A stricter permanence test for new applicants.** Impairments won't count as permanent unless you've "undertaken all appropriate treatment" and they're "likely to persist for the person's lifetime". "Appropriate treatment" must be evidence-based, able to "reliably be expected to materially improve, reverse, or alleviate" the impairment, and regularly performed in Australia. Existing participants will be progressively reassessed over three years.{% ref "ndis-amend-2026" %} |

**What this means for ME/CFS:** the Government says "you will never be expected to undergo treatment that ... is not recommended for your impairment ... or could cause serious long-term side effects".{% ref "health-ndis-changes" %} Emerge Australia's view is that because no ME/CFS treatment meets all three "appropriate treatment" tests, the 2028 rules risk leaving people in "an indefinite limbo of expected treatment compliance with nothing to comply with", and it is lobbying for the guidance to say so explicitly.{% ref "emerge-ndis-submission-2026" %} If you're thinking of applying, **the current rules apply until 1 January 2028.**

## How to apply, step by step

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Get help first (optional but wise).</strong> A Local Area Coordinator can help you gather evidence and complete the form. Find one via the NDIS locations page or call <a href="tel:1800800110">1800 800 110</a>.{% ref "ndis-how-apply", "ndis-contact" %} Emerge Australia runs a <strong>free online module, "Applying to the NDIS"</strong>, with worksheets and a template letter for your doctor, plus live Q&A sessions.{% ref "emerge-ndis" %}</li>
<li><strong>Book a long GP appointment.</strong> Go through the criteria together, and ask whether any further treatment should be tried before you apply, since the NDIS wants evidence you've "tried all available treatment options".{% ref "emerge-ndis-factsheet-2025" %}</li>
<li><strong>Gather evidence.</strong> The NDIS wants a recent report from a treating health professional who has treated you for at least 6 months, confirming your primary disability, the impact on the six areas of life, previous treatments and outcomes, and future treatment options.{% ref "ndis-evidence" %} Specialist reports carry extra weight, but a GP's is acceptable. Accepted formats include existing reports, letters, the NDIS supporting evidence form, and standard tools such as the <strong>WHODAS 2.0</strong>.{% ref "ndis-evidence-examples" %}</li>
<li><strong>Get a functional capacity assessment if you can.</strong> An occupational therapist's FCA looks at your ability to do daily tasks at home and in the community across the six areas, and OTs are experienced at writing NDIS reports.{% ref "ndis-fca", "emerge-ndis-factsheet-2025" %} The NDIS doesn't fund this before you're a participant; some OT input can be Medicare-subsidised through a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan. ME Group Australia publishes a <strong>guide for OTs on assessing people with ME</strong> and a list of ME-aware OTs.{% ref "mega-ot-guide" %} If you can't get an FCA, ask your doctor to describe your function in each area, and add a <strong>personal statement and a carer statement</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Complete the Access Request Form.</strong> Your section covers identity, consent and your disability; your health professional completes the evidence section including functional capacity in each of the six areas. Submit through the NDIS service hub, by post (NDIA, GPO Box 700, Canberra ACT 2601) or in person.{% ref "ndis-arf" %} You can also apply by phone on 1800 800 110.</li>
<li><strong>Wait for the decision.</strong> The NDIA's service guarantee is a decision within <strong>90 days</strong> of receiving your application and all evidence, or 14 days after any extra information it requests.{% ref "ndis-psg" %} If you're eligible you'll get an impairment notice; if not, a letter explaining why.</li>
</ol>

### Framing tips from ME/CFS organisations

- **Use the name "myalgic encephalomyelitis (ME)"** throughout, not "chronic fatigue syndrome", which is often confused with the *symptom* of chronic fatigue.{% ref "emerge-ndis-factsheet-2023" %}
- **Ask your doctor to state that GET and CBT are not best practice** for ME/CFS and are likely to worsen it. Without this, an assessor may treat them as "available treatment" you haven't tried.{% ref "emerge-ndis-factsheet-2023" %}
- **Describe each of the six areas concretely:** how far you can walk before resting, what aids you use, whether you can cook, clean, manage bills and appointments, hold a conversation, or tolerate heat and cold.{% ref "emerge-ndis-gp" %}
- **Describe the aftermath, not just the activity:** what happens the day after a shower, an outing or an appointment.
- **Make sure the primary disability is recorded as physical or neurological ("Other")**, not psychosocial.{% ref "mega-ndis-access" %}
- **Include everything comorbid:** POTS, sleep disorders, MCAS, hypermobility, pain.
- **Don't wait for a good week to be assessed**, and say clearly that a good day isn't recovery.

## What the NDIS funds for ME/CFS

Since October 2024, plan funds can only be spent on items on the official "NDIS supports" list.{% ref "ndis-supports-list" %} Supports commonly used by people with ME/CFS include:{% ref "ndis-supports-list", "emerge-ndis-factsheet-2025", "me-primer-ndis" %}

- **Support workers** for personal care (showering, dressing, eating) and for going out
- **Household tasks** you can't do because of your disability: cleaning, laundry, meal preparation, yard maintenance, or meal delivery
- **Assistive equipment:** shower chairs, recliners, wheelchairs, mobility scooters, transfer aids and hoists, adjustable beds, laptop stands, noise-cancelling headphones and dark glasses for sensory sensitivity
- **Home modifications:** grab rails, ramps, wider doorways
- **Transport** if you can't use public transport independently
- **Allied health**, such as an occupational therapist for pacing and energy management, physiotherapy, psychology or a dietitian, delivered at home or by telehealth
- **Support coordination** to manage the plan for you
- **Replacement supports:** with approval, plan funds can buy standard items such as a smartwatch, tablet or dishwasher if they replace a support in your plan at the same or lower cost{% ref "ndis-replacement" %}

**Not funded:** anything that counts as health care rather than disability support. That includes doctors, specialists, diagnosis and treatment, hospital care, **prescription and non-prescription medicines, vitamins and supplements**, and everyday living costs like rent, groceries and standard household appliances. Alternative therapies (aromatherapy, reflexology, "wellness coaches" and the like) are explicitly excluded.{% ref "ndis-supports-list" %}

## If you're refused

Refusals are common for ME/CFS. ME/CFS Australia says freedom-of-information data show a rejection rate above 60%, though the document isn't public,{% ref "mecfs-au-issues" %} and Emerge Australia notes that people rejected at first often gain access on appeal.{% ref "emerge-ndis-position" %}

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Internal review.</strong> Ask within <strong>3 months</strong> of receiving the decision, by phone (1800 800 110), through the service hub, using the "Request for a review of a decision" form, or by post to the CEO, NDIA, GPO Box 700, Canberra ACT 2601. The NDIA aims to complete reviews within 60 days.{% ref "ndis-review" %} This is your chance to add the evidence that was missing: a functional capacity assessment, a stronger doctor's letter, carer statements.</li>
<li><strong>Administrative Review Tribunal.</strong> If the internal review fails, apply to the ART within <strong>28 days</strong> (extensions can be requested). There's no fee. If the NDIA hasn't finished the internal review within 90 days, the ART may take the case anyway. Phone 1800 228 333 or email reviews@art.gov.au.{% ref "art-ndis" %}</li>
<li><strong>Get a free advocate.</strong> The <strong>NDIS Appeals Program</strong> provides a free advocate to support you through an ART review, and Legal Aid can sometimes represent you.{% ref "ndis-appeals-program" %}</li>
<li><strong>Or reapply.</strong> You can lodge a new application with new evidence at any time.</li>
</ol>

## If you're not eligible

- **Disability Support Pension:** a separate system with different rules. You can claim both. See [Centrelink & the DSP](/support/centrelink/).{% ref "sa-dsp-who" %}
- **Support at Home / My Aged Care** if you're 65 or over (50 or over for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people): [1800 200 422](tel:1800200422).{% ref "myagedcare-eligible" %}
- **Carer Gateway** for the person caring for you: [1800 422 737](tel:1800422737).
- **Foundational supports:** a new $10 billion programme of supports outside the NDIS, starting with children from October 2026. No start date has been announced for adult "targeted supports".{% ref "foundational-supports" %}
- **Medicare:** a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan for subsidised allied health.
- **State ME/CFS organisations** may know local programmes.{% ref "mecfs-au-members" %}

- **NDIS:** [1800 800 110](tel:1800800110), Monday–Friday 8am–7pm; interpreters 131 450; National Relay Service.{% ref "ndis-contact" %}
- **Emerge Australia:** free "Applying to the NDIS" module, template letter, Q&A sessions, and nurse-led telehealth support on [1800 865 321](tel:1800865321). They can't provide individual advocacy or write reports.{% ref "emerge-ndis" %}
- **ME Group Australia:** NDIS guides, an OT guide and ME-aware OT list.{% ref "mega-ot-guide", "mega-ndis-access" %}
- **Disability Advocacy Support Helpline:** [1800 643 787](tel:1800643787) for short-term advocacy.{% ref "advocacy-helpline" %}

- **NDIS Appeals Program** for Tribunal reviews.{% ref "ndis-appeals-program" %}
